01 / CHOOSE YOUR SIZE

Start with where it belongs.

Measure the person, sofa or bed you’re making for. Presets are editable examples: baby 30 × 36 in, lap 36 × 48, throw 50 × 60, twin 66 × 90, queen 90 × 100.

02 / ACCOUNT FOR BORDERS

Leave a little room at the edges.

Enter dimensions for the main fabric only. For a 50 × 60 inch blanket with a 2-inch border on every side, plan a 46 × 56 inch main panel.

03 / CHECK YOUR GAUGE

Big projects magnify small differences.

A difference of half a stitch per inch adds up over a wide blanket. Measure after washing and drying, and recheck your width after the first few rows.

Gauge reference: Craft Yarn Council · How to measure. Examples and calculations on this page are our own.

BEFORE YOU CAST ON

A few loose
ends, tied up.

Good questions make good projects.

✳
How many stitches do I need for a 50-inch blanket?+

At a gauge of 18 stitches per 4 inches, 50 inches needs 225 stitches before adjusting to a pattern repeat. At 24 rows per 4 inches, a 60-inch length needs 360 rows. Your yarn, tension and stitch pattern determine your actual counts.

Does the result include turning chains?+

No. The result describes stitches in the fabric. Your pattern may call for additional foundation or turning chains, and may count a turning chain as a stitch. Follow the pattern rather than adding the same chain count to every project.

Are the preset sizes official standards?+

No. They are practical starting examples, not universal sizes or safety guidance. Bed dimensions, mattress depth and desired drape vary. Measure your intended use and edit the dimensions.

Can it estimate how much yarn I need?+

This version calculates stitches, rows and projected fabric size. It does not estimate yardage: that requires measured yarn consumption, stitch pattern, fiber, border and finishing allowances.

Does this work for granny-square blankets?+

It assumes a rectangular panel worked in regular rows at a consistent gauge. Granny squares, corner-to-corner, chevrons and diagonal constructions need construction-specific calculations.
