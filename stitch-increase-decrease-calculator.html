A FEW THINGS WORTH KNOWING

Keep the count. Find the rhythm.

Simple formulas. A little understanding.
More time for the good part.

01 / COUNT THE CHANGE

The difference sets the plan.

110 − 100 = 10 increases. An increase uses one old stitch and creates two new stitches. A two-stitch decrease uses two old stitches and creates one.

02 / SPREAD IT OUT

Space the shaping across the row.

We divide the plain stitches into gaps before, between and after the shaping points. Gaps differ by at most one stitch. This is a balanced spacing plan, not pattern-specific garment shaping.

03 / CHECK THE ROW

Every old stitch has a home.

The row must consume exactly your starting count and produce exactly the target. Changes too large for one row are rejected so you can spread them across several rows instead.

BEFORE YOU CAST ON

A few loose
ends, tied up.

Good questions make good projects.

✳
How do I increase evenly from 100 to 110 stitches?+

Make 10 increases, each using 1 old stitch to produce 2. That leaves 90 plain stitches, distributed across the 11 gaps before, between and after those increases. The sequence uses all 100 starting stitches and finishes with 110.

How do I decrease evenly from 100 to 90 stitches?+

Make 10 decreases. They consume 20 old stitches and produce 10 new stitches, leaving 80 stitches to work normally. We distribute those 80 across 11 gaps for a balanced row.

What do the crochet instructions mean?+

US single crochet (sc) is the plain stitch. An increase is 2 sc into the next stitch; a decrease is sc2tog over the next 2 stitches. A UK double crochet corresponds to US single crochet. Turning chains are excluded and are worked according to your pattern.

Which knitting increase is used?+

The instructions use kfb (knit front and back), which consumes one stitch and produces two. Decreases use k2tog. M1 increases use the strand between stitches and require a different spacing method; do not substitute M1 directly into this sequence.

Can I keep edge stitches or work in the round?+

The sequence uses the full row count and allows shaping at the edges when plain gaps are zero. For reserved edge stitches, calculate only the interior and add the reserved stitches back yourself. Circular knitting, mirrored decreases and motif placement need a pattern-specific plan.
